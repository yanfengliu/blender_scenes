## Reports

### Independent Codex reviewer

Accept this as useful six-path geometric feasibility evidence and accept making lower length an explicit design variable. Do not accept a complete groom, unchanged-clearance equivalence, or natural appearance from this experiment. Five adjacent historical guide paths and one exact failed seeded child are a meaningful local failure neighborhood. Three geometry-derived cut heights and two corridor constructions provide a bounded comparison without concealing the unsuccessful properties of alternatives. They do not cover the other guide cells, sides, moving child interpolation, strand widths or all 23,000 children.

The owner's requested outcome is natural hair with the existing visual benchmark and real body/clothing/face/ear protection. The earlier 0.3% total-length and 25 micrometre per-edge preservation restrictions were choices of the rejected local repair mechanism, not an expressed requirement to preserve the lower haircut exactly. Requiring them indefinitely would confuse the prior algorithm with the intended result. It is reasonable to retire them explicitly while preserving the failed records, reporting length and endpoint changes, and continuing to judge the full silhouette at native resolution. This is a changed design contract; it does not retroactively make the local solver pass its former contract.

The proposed original-height nearest-exterior route adds 17.37 to 20.42 mm of total arc length, or 4.70% to 5.56% of each complete curve. The fixed prefix dilutes that percentage: my independent calculation gives 12.65% to 16.30% added length in the modified lower tail alone. Keeping the original tip height does not preserve its horizontal position, arc length or visual haircut. These quantities should remain explicit in the next full-field comparison. The geometry-derived shorter cuts are legitimate alternatives to record, but their tighter guide concentration does not make them preferable merely because they shorten the hair.

The support construction is substantially stronger than the previously incomplete visible-body check. `probe_global_route.py:82-91` loads the saved candidate's actual native coordinates, rebuilds the complete canonical face population through `full_body_support`, and evaluates that complete support. The unchanged `scenes/body_proportions.py:149-174` uses the complete topology at subdivision level two and cleans up its temporary object/mesh. The receipt records 19,158 source vertices, 13,378 canonical body faces and 214,048 evaluated body faces. It also records 20,574 evaluated clothing polygons overlapping the lower band. This addresses clothing-covered body triangles rather than assuming their absence from the visible mesh means free space.

The continuous spline certificate has a sound geometric basis within the represented support domain. `global_route_core.py:111-118` conservatively includes all vertices of faces whose height ranges overlap each dilated slab. A support half-plane formed from their maximal projection bounds those faces. The cubic B-spline has eight spans, while the 32 certificate intervals stay within its knot spans. `:36-61` converts each interval to cubic Bezier controls; constraining all four controls outside the same plane constrains their entire convex combination. The constrained quadratic solver explicitly checks its final primal inequalities and boundary equalities rather than relying only on convergence status. The position, tangent and second-derivative match is to the quadratic fitted through the last three preserved POLY points. It is not a claim that the original polygonal curve has classical curvature continuity at a vertex. The separate measured join turns are therefore necessary and meaningful.

The actual emitted centerlines are checked separately from the smooth mathematical curve. The selected set retains 110 points for each guide and 64 for the child. The lower checker clips segments through the height-band boundaries instead of checking only vertices already inside the band. Its actual ray crossings and inside-body samples detect the original six failed witnesses and reject them before fitting; the selected routes have zero recorded body or clothing crossings and zero recorded inside-body samples over 190 relevant actual segments, including six boundary-crossing segments. The 513-sample dense paths are supplementary evidence, not a substitute for these actual polylines. Unchanged upper face/ear guards and preserved prefix coordinates also remain meaningful local checks.

The strongest selected route keeps the lower maximum absolute X at 82.470 mm and its maximum modified turn at 6.329 degrees. The original-cut radial alternative reaches 208.130 mm in absolute X and adds roughly 60.55 to 65.55 mm of arc length. This is useful counterevidence against that radial corridor for the intended silhouette. Neither numerical comparison establishes that the nearest-exterior result looks natural; no integrated render was made for this instrument.

F21: the current six-path evidence does not preserve the former 5 mm lower guide target. Its continuous cubic support margin is 4.5 mm, and the separately audited actual POLY minimum is 4.4853286 mm. The latter is about 14.67 micrometres below 4.5 mm and must not be rounded into an exact 4.5 mm emitted-chord assertion. The old local solver used `margin=.005 if guide else .0015` in `lower-3d/constraint-source.py:147`, while its separate verification chiefly rejected crossings and inside samples. The new source initially established zero crossings and the stated support margins, not actual body-and-clothing distance equivalence to the old projection target. Its revised handoff correctly limits that claim. A length-contract correction does not by itself waive a clearance contract.

Root explicitly accepted this distinction and directed the unexecuted next instrument to retain a 5 mm actual guide margin and a 1.5 mm actual child margin to both complete body and clothing, preserve existing upper guards, and certify emitted chords. The hair author separately acknowledged this and proposed 6 mm construction padding with actual-distance failures closed. This is a future requirement and proposed construction, not a runtime pass. The report does not reinterpret the existing 4.485 mm result as a 5 mm result.

There is also a support-domain qualification to address before using the certificate as a complete full-field proof. `probe_global_route.py:92-96` first filters clothing to the undilated overall lower band, while the later slab calculation expands each interval vertically by 4.5 mm. Near a global boundary, a clothing face outside that initial band could belong to a padded slab but already have been excluded. I found no demonstrated missed collision in the selected paths; the issue is the completeness of the stronger padded-domain claim. The next instrument should retain all relevant evaluated clothing or apply consistent initial padding, record the evaluated modifier levels used, and assert the intended support population before certifying distances. The current complete-body reconstruction does not have that clothing-prefilter limitation.

The neighbor analysis correctly exposes a remaining physical problem. `analyze_global_neighbors.py:13-25` tests segment-pair distance through the interior stationary candidate and all four parameter boundaries; it is not merely comparing endpoints. It tests all ten pairs among the five selected guide polylines. There is no detected pair intersection within its 1 micrometre threshold, but guides 42/43 approach to 0.608843 mm, versus 2.845068 mm before fitting, retaining only about 21.4% of their former minimum separation. This is substantial concentration. The nearest-exterior midpoint and higher-cut alternatives are tighter still. The exact seeded child is not a population-wide child/guide or child/child spacing proof.

The selected nearest-exterior paths also invert angular order at 90 of 401 common-height samples. Angular order alone is not a valid collision criterion when paths occupy different radii, so treating it as descriptive is justified. The raw receipt retains the original coarse group rule, under which those nearest-exterior groups fail its order condition; `assessment.json` explicitly supersedes that coarse status with six-path feasibility only. The revised interpretation must preserve this history and the actual spacing evidence. Dropping an angular proxy is not a reason to drop a physical spacing or child-clearance check.

The proposed wave/radius budget is useful only conditionally. The stated ordinary and loose maximum XY wave norms, 0.6414 and 1.4577 mm, plus an 0.080 mm maximum shaft radius leave approximately 3.764 and 2.948 mm against the recorded support planes. The measured radial-offset direction is outward for these selected paths. Independent guides need not share a support plane, however, and production children move between guide indices as they fall. Convex interpolation of separately safe points does not preserve either point's distinct corridor. Neither the scalar subtraction nor the five-guide nonintersection result certifies the actual interpolated field or its visible density. Existing downward endpoint jitter also leaves a fixed-height corridor unless replaced by truncation along a certified path or separately recertified.

The next evidence-only implementation should therefore first establish shared support corridors and usable transverse spacing across actual guide cells, then apply the unchanged ordinary/loose populations, layers and bounded offsets, and finally inspect every actual emitted child segment with widths accounted for. It must reject insufficient body or clothing distance, upper-guard failures, new crossings, sharp reversals and collapsed segments. It must report actual guide/child minimum distances, chord margins, length/end changes and runtime cost. A proposed shared corridor is not itself evidence that these tests will pass. Only after those checks and integration into the actual intended scene can fixed and held-out native views establish whether the route improves the helmet shape, lower shoulder contact and hair naturalness.
